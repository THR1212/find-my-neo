Imagine you walk into an enormous ice cream shop with 500 confusing jars and toppings. The cashier points to a giant menu board full of complicated packages: "Bronze Cup: 15g sugar, Silver Cone: 50g sugar, Gold Tub: unlimited napkins."

You have no idea what to choose! You just want a tasty snack for yourself and your baby sister. If you pick the giant Gold Tub, you spend all your pocket money and feel sick. If you pick the tiny Bronze Cup, you run out of ice cream in two bites and get upset.

Find My Neo is a friendly shop assistant who steps out with a smile and says: "Tell me what you're craving!" You say: "I like chocolate, and my sister is small." The assistant asks two quick questions ("Cones or cups? Any sprinkles?"), hands you the exact right double-scoop cone, gives you a sticker with your name on it, tells you the exact price, and walks you right to the cashier. Q1
Isn't this just a form with extra steps?
Design / Product
🎯 What the Judge Is Really Thinking
"Why didn't you just make a Typeform or Google Form? Aren't you over-engineering something simple to look flashy?"
🗣️ The Official Pro Answer
"A form asks everyone the same things. This picks each question by how much it would change the recommendation, and stops the moment nothing left can change it — so the locksmith answers fewer questions than the studio. And it's a form that ends in a price, not a category."
🧸 Explain Like I'm 5 (The 20 Questions Game)
A regular form is like a boring school test where every single kid has to answer all 50 questions, even the questions about dinosaurs when you're taking a math test!

Find My Neo is playing the game "Guess Who?" or "20 Questions". If your first clue is "My character has a giant white beard", you don't ask "Are they wearing earrings?". You immediately ask: "Are they Santa Claus?" The second you know it's Santa, the game is over! A simple solo locksmith might only answer 3 quick questions. A big photo studio with 4K video files and invoicing might get 5. And it gives you an exact price tag, not just a tag saying "You're a business!"
🔍 Plain-English Jargon Buster
Discrimination Score How good a question is at cutting the remaining options in half. If a question doesn't eliminate any plans, we throw it in the trash and don't ask it.
Early Stop Code checking after every single tap: "Could any remaining question change what plan this person gets?" If no, STOP asking and show the prize!
🕵️ The Behind-the-Scenes Secret (From the Code)
In src/lib/engine.ts, the math function discrimination() scores every question against remaining candidate plans. Questions that only change cosmetic feature bullets are capped by FEATURE_ONLY_BUDGET = 2 so we never trap the user in question purgatory.
10-Sec Panic Line "Forms ask everyone everything; we play Guess Who, cut questions that don't move the price, and stop the millisecond the plan is settled."

Q3
What if it recommends the wrong plan?
Customer Support / QA
🎯 What the Judge Is Really Thinking
"AI makes stuff up (hallucinations). If an LLM tells a customer to buy the wrong plan, our support team will get yelled at and have to issue refunds!"
🗣️ The Official Pro Answer
"The plan isn't a judgement call — it's the cheapest configuration that satisfies every requirement the answers established, and each requirement points at a specific Neo entitlement. If it's wrong, it's wrong the same way every time and we can trace exactly which answer caused it. That's the reason we didn't let the model decide it."
🧸 Explain Like I'm 5 (The Strict Lego Box Rule)
Imagine you're building a Lego castle. You don't ask a magic talking robot: "Hey robot, guess what box I should buy!" The robot might say "Buy the $500 spaceship box because it looks shiny!" That's silly.

Instead, you write down on paper: "I need 4 wheels and 2 windows." Then a simple metal sorting machine looks at the rulebook and finds the smallest, cheapest box that contains 4 wheels and 2 windows. If the box is wrong, it's because you wrote down the wrong number of wheels—not because the robot had a daydream! The AI never picks the plan; strict math code does.
🔍 Plain-English Jargon Buster
Entitlement A feature or permission stored in Neo's database (e.g., "Can this user send 1,000 emails a day?", "Do they have Neo Drive storage?").
Deterministic 100% predictable math. 2 + 2 will equal 4 every single time, whether you run it today, tomorrow, or a million times.
🕵️ The Behind-the-Scenes Secret (From the Code)
In src/lib/rules.ts and candidates.ts, plans are mapped strictly from requirements to Pandora database entitlements. If someone needs Appointment Booking, code flags appointment_booking, which sets the floor to Max. The AI cannot invent entitlements or pick plans.
10-Sec Panic Line "The plan isn't an AI guess—it's a strict mathematical lookup of the cheapest plan that covers their required entitlements, fully traceable in code."

Q9
Does the AI pick the price?
Architecture
🎯 What the Judge Is Really Thinking
"Did you let an unconstrained LLM output numbers like 'Price: $19/mo'? What happens when prompt injection makes it offer a plan for ₹0?"
🗣️ The Official Pro Answer
"No, and it can't — it is never given the numbers. Prices and plan limits live in code. The model may only raise a tier, never lower one, and only by naming a specific entitlement and quoting the customer's own words — which the server then checks against what they actually wrote. A quote it can't find is a raise that gets refused."
🧸 Explain Like I'm 10 (The 4 Security Guard Checks)
Imagine you visit a carnival game. The AI is a carnival barker who listens to you. But the AI is blindfolded and has never seen any money in its life! It doesn't even know what a Rupee or a Dollar is.

If the AI thinks you need a higher plan because of something unique you typed, it has to pass 4 strict security guards on our server:
  1. Check 1 (The Password Enum): The AI can only name a pre-approved entitlement from an official list (like drive_storage). It cannot make up a new word.
  2. Check 2 (The Price Table): The server looks up the price in our own hardcoded file. The AI's opinion on price is never asked.
  3. Check 3 (The Verbatim Quote Check): The AI must quote the exact words the user wrote. If the user wrote "I send huge 4K movies" and the AI quotes "I send huge 4K movies", the server checks if those characters exist in the user's text. If not, the raise is rejected!
  4. Check 4 (Tap Beats AI): If the user explicitly tapped a button on screen, the tap overrules the AI every single time!
🔍 Plain-English Jargon Buster
Enum / Constrained Decoding Forcing the AI's output format so it can only pick from a fixed set of approved keys. It is physically impossible for the model to output a made-up word.
Verbatim Substring Match Server code doing text.includes(quote) to make sure the AI isn't inventing fake quotes.
🕵️ The Behind-the-Scenes Secret (From the Code)
In api/_lib/planService.ts: a video studio typed "enormous 4K video files every single day" but tapped "Mostly just messages" on the volume question. The model attempted to raise them to Max. The server rejected the raise because ENTITLEMENT_QUESTION['drive_storage'] = 'volume' was already tapped!
10-Sec Panic Line "The AI never sees numbers; it can only request a tier raise by citing a fixed enum and verbatim customer quote, which our server strictly validates."
Q10
What does the model actually do, then?
AI Engineering
🎯 What the Judge Is Really Thinking
"If deterministic code handles the plan, pricing, and rules, why did you even use an LLM? Is the AI just a marketing buzzword?"
🗣️ The Official Pro Answer
"Three things: it reads the free-text description into a structured profile, it rewrites each question and its options for that specific business, and it writes the explanation on the reveal from reasons the solver already computed. Everything that costs money is deterministic; everything that makes it feel written for you is generated."
🧸 Explain Like I'm 5 (The Poet and the Accountant)
Imagine you're planning a birthday party. You hire two people:

1. The Accountant (Deterministic Code): Super strict, calculates the exact bill, checks how many chairs are in the hall, and guarantees you don't overspend a single dime.
2. The Poet / Friendly Host (The AI Model): Listens to your favorite stories, designs personalized party invitations that mention your love of puppies, and writes a heartfelt birthday card just for you!

If the Poet tried to do the math, they'd make mistakes. If the Accountant tried to write the party card, it would look like a boring spreadsheet. The AI handles the empathy and personalization; the code handles the money.
🔍 Plain-English Jargon Buster
Structured Profile Converting messy human sentences ("we sell custom cakes in Mumbai") into neat computer data ({ industry: "bakery", location: "Mumbai", teamSize: 2 }).
Dynamic Rewording Changing generic questions like "Do you sell products?" into "Do your cakes ship across India or just local delivery?" so the user feels understood.
🕵️ The Behind-the-Scenes Secret (From the Code)
In src/lib/api.ts and api/questions.ts, the LLM takes the fixed question bank from questions.ts and generates a SurfaceMap. If the model is offline, the app simply uses the fixed questions without blinking!
10-Sec Panic Line "The LLM does empathy, not arithmetic: it extracts the profile, customizes question wording, and writes the reveal explanation based on solver facts."
Q11
How do you choose which question to ask next?
Algorithm / Math
🎯 What the Judge Is Really Thinking
"Is it just asking questions 1, 2, 3, 4 in a fixed order? Or is the AI hallucinating random questions out of nowhere?"
🗣️ The Official Pro Answer
"Each remaining question is scored by how many candidate plans it would eliminate. We ask the most discriminating one, and stop when nothing left would change the outcome. That's also why two businesses get different questions rather than the same list reordered."
🧸 Explain Like I'm 10 (The Binary Search Tree / 20 Questions)
Suppose I pick a number between 1 and 100. If your first question is: "Is it 47?", that's a terrible question! If I say no, you only eliminated 1 number and 99 numbers remain.

A genius player asks: "Is it higher than 50?" Boom! Half the universe (50 numbers) is eliminated in one move!

Our question engine does the exact same thing with computer plans:
  • Before each screen, code asks: "If the user answers Question A, how many plans get eliminated? What about Question B?"
  • We rank them: Price Impact first, then Feature bullets second, then Model preference only as a tie-breaker.
  • We ask the question that slashes the possibilities the most.
🔍 Plain-English Jargon Buster
Lexicographic Ranking Sorting by priority levels. First priority: Does it change what they pay? Second priority: Does it change what features they see? Third priority: AI tie-breaker.
Discrimination The mathematical power of an answer to rule out candidate choices.
🕵️ The Behind-the-Scenes Secret (From the Code)
On 03 Sep (documented in DECISIONS.md), we caught a bug where the model was asking "Which mail client do you use?" early because it swapped two feature bullets. We fixed it with lexicographic ranking: price-moving questions are strictly forced ahead of cosmetic feature questions!
10-Sec Panic Line "We score each question by how many candidate plans it mathematically eliminates; price-deciding questions are prioritized over cosmetic feature questions."
Q12
What happens when the model call fails or times out?
Resilience / DevOps
🎯 What the Judge Is Really Thinking
"OpenAI goes down all the time. If an API request times out or returns a 500 error, does the entire checkout page crash with a white screen of death?"
🗣️ The Official Pro Answer
"Nothing user-visible breaks. Questions fall back to their fixed wording and the recommendation is unaffected, because the plan never depended on the model. The harder problem was that this fails silently — a timeout returns a plausible screen, not an error — so we added reporting for the empty-but-successful case specifically."
🧸 Explain Like I'm 5 (The Backup Candle)
Imagine you're having dinner at a restaurant with fancy electric lights that change colors. Suddenly, the power company has a blackout!

The waiter doesn't throw your food in the trash and kick you out into the rain! Instead, the restaurant instantly lights warm, beautiful candles on your table. You still eat your delicious pizza, the food tastes the exact same, and you leave completely happy.

In Find My Neo, every external API call has a safety net (Graceful Degradation). If OpenAI times out, the app silently uses our standard, beautifully written question bank. The plan calculation doesn't care about OpenAI anyway! The user never sees an error popup.
🔍 Plain-English Jargon Buster
Graceful Degradation When something breaks behind the scenes, the website keeps running smoothly instead of showing an error screen.
Fallback Bank The pre-written list of standard questions stored safely inside the frontend bundle.
🕵️ The Behind-the-Scenes Secret (From the Code)
In api/_lib/llm.ts and api/log.ts, failures don't throw uncaught exceptions. Because a timeout silently returns default wording without crashing, we built custom telemetry in api/log.ts to log "empty-but-successful" events so engineers can track OpenAI uptime.
10-Sec Panic Line "Zero crashes: timeouts fall back to our pre-packaged question bank and the plan calculation proceeds identically, with silent errors logged to telemetry."

Which model, and why that one?
Model Selection / Cost

If you just need to ride down the street to buy a bottle of milk, you don't rent a NASA Space Shuttle rocket! A rocket takes 3 hours to fuel up and costs millions of dollars. You jump on a swift, lightweight bicycle and get there in 30 seconds.

We picked GPT-5.6 (Terra/Luna) because it responds in a split-second. And because we wrapped it in a clean adapter, swapping to another model (like Claude, Gemini, or Llama) is as simple as changing one line in a settings file!

🕵️ The Behind-the-Scenes Secret (From the Code)
In TECHNICAL.md: Terra was already A/B proven in flock-partner-analysis. Luna is 10x cheaper ($0.20/1M tokens) and ideal for production. The model is called strictly via api/_lib/llm.ts; changing MODEL_NAME in config points the entire pipeline to any model.
10-Sec Panic Line "GPT-5.6 selected for low latency rather than deep reasoning; decoupled behind a 1-line config seam so we can swap providers instantly."
Q15

If you only checked City Hall, you would take your suitcases, try to unlock the door, and crash into someone's living room! That's what happens on the web: a domain can be free on the public internet, but already reserved inside Neo's private order system. We check BOTH sources so the customer never hits a broken checkout.

📖 Master Jargon Decoder

Plain-English definitions for every buzzword used in this repository
🔑 Pandora
Neo's internal backend authority for accounts and feature entitlements. If the marketing table says X and Pandora says Y, Pandora always wins.
🎯 Entitlement
A specific permission stored in Neo's database (e.g. drive_storage, signature_builder). A plan is simply a bundled basket of entitlements.
🎲 Deterministic
Math that produces the exact same answer every single time. Putting 2 + 2 into a calculator is deterministic; rolling dice or asking an LLM to guess a price is not.
📉 Churn Rate
The percentage of paying customers who cancel their subscription within a given time period (e.g., month 3). High churn destroys software company value.
⚡ Discrimination Score
A mathematical calculation measuring how many candidate plans a question rules out. The question with the highest score is asked next.
🛡️ Graceful Degradation
Engineering software so that if a third-party service (like OpenAI) goes down, the app silently falls back to pre-packaged data without crashing or showing an error.
